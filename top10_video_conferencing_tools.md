## Detailed Tool Profiles

---

### 1. Zoom
**Website:** https://zoom.us/
**Starting Price:** Free (40-minute limit on group calls) / ~$13.33/month (Pro, annual)

**Overview:**
Zoom became the defining video conferencing platform of the remote work era. With 300 million daily meeting participants at its peak, its reliability, feature depth, and cross-platform consistency made it the universal default for business and personal video calls alike.

**Best For:**
- Any team that needs a universally recognized, reliable video platform
- Webinars and virtual events (up to 50,000 attendees on enterprise plans)
- Hybrid work environments mixing in-person and remote participants

**Strengths:**
- Best-in-class audio and video quality and reliability
- Breakout rooms, polls, reactions, and whiteboard built in
- Zoom AI Companion (meeting summaries, chat assistance)
- Massive ecosystem of Zoom Apps and marketplace integrations
- Up to 1,000 participants on large meeting add-ons

**Limitations:**
- Free plan limits group meetings to 40 minutes
- Security concerns surfaced in 2020 (most have since been resolved)
- Can feel heavyweight compared to lighter tools like Google Meet

---

### 2. Microsoft Teams
**Website:** https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-teams/
**Starting Price:** Free / ~$4/user/month (Microsoft 365 Business Basic, annual)

**Overview:**
Microsoft Teams is the dominant video conferencing and collaboration platform for enterprise environments running Microsoft 365. It combines video meetings, persistent team chat, file storage (SharePoint), and Office app co-editing into a single workspace.

**Best For:**
- Enterprises and organizations running Microsoft 365
- Teams that heavily use Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and SharePoint
- Education institutions (Teams for Education is free for schools)

**Strengths:**
- Deepest Microsoft 365 integration (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OneNote, SharePoint)
- Teams Phone (PSTN calls) for full telephony replacement
- Together Mode for immersive group presence
- Up to 1,000 participants in meetings; 10,000 in Town Halls
- Copilot AI for meeting transcription, summaries, and action items

**Limitations:**
- Interface is complex and requires onboarding time
- Notification system can become overwhelming
- Performance can lag on older hardware

---

### 3. Google Meet
**Website:** https://meet.google.com/
**Starting Price:** Free / included in Google Workspace (~$6/user/month)

**Overview:**
Google Meet is the simplest and most browser-native video conferencing tool available. No download required — join from any browser via link. For Google Workspace organizations, it's already included and integrates seamlessly into Calendar, Gmail, and Drive.

**Best For:**
- Google Workspace users who want zero-friction video calls
- Quick ad-hoc meetings without software installation
- Education (Google Meet is free and integrated with Google Classroom)

**Strengths:**
- Zero install — entirely browser-based for participants
- Native integration with Google Calendar (meeting link auto-created)
- Real-time captions and live translation powered by Google AI
- Included free in all Google Workspace plans
- Up to 1,000 participants on Enterprise Workspace

**Limitations:**
- Free tier limits calls to 60 minutes (1 hour vs Zoom's 40 minutes)
- Fewer advanced features than Zoom or Teams (no breakout rooms on free)
- Recording requires a paid Google Workspace plan

---

### 4. Webex
**Website:** https://www.webex.com/
**Starting Price:** Free / ~$14.50/user/month (Meet plan, annual)

**Overview:**
Webex by Cisco is the enterprise-grade video conferencing and webinar platform with the deepest security and compliance capabilities. Its AI-powered Webex Assistant handles real-time transcription, translation, meeting summaries, and action items across 100+ languages.

**Best For:**
- Government agencies and regulated industries (HIPAA, FedRAMP, ISO)
- Large-scale webinars and virtual events
- Enterprises with Cisco networking infrastructure

**Strengths:**
- Best security and compliance certifications in the industry
- Webex Events supports up to 100,000 attendees
- AI Assistant: transcription, translations, and meeting summaries in 100+ languages
- Webex Slido (audience Q&A and polling) built in
- Hardware-agnostic with Webex Rooms for in-person integration

**Limitations:**
- Interface is less intuitive than Zoom for first-time users
- More expensive than Zoom for equivalent features
- Free tier is less capable than Zoom's free offering

---

### 5. GoTo Meeting
**Website:** https://www.goto.com/meeting
**Starting Price:** ~$12/user/month (Professional, annual billing)

**Overview:**
GoTo Meeting (formerly Citrix GoToMeeting) has served business meeting needs since 2004. Focused purely on business meetings rather than team collaboration, it excels in simplicity, call quality, and the Smart Meeting feature that records and transcribes meetings automatically.

**Best For:**
- Small and mid-size businesses wanting reliable business meetings
- Teams that want meeting transcripts and recordings without complexity
- Users who need a dedicated meeting tool separate from chat platforms

**Strengths:**
- Smart Meeting: auto-transcription and action item detection
- Strong audio quality with noise suppression
- Commuter Mode (audio-only to save mobile data)
- Simple, no-frills interface focused on meetings
- No participant download required for joining

**Limitations:**
- No free tier (only 14-day trial)
- Lacks the breadth of features in Zoom or Teams
- Ecosystem and integrations smaller than major platforms

### 6. RingCentral Video
**Website:** https://www.ringcentral.com/video-meetings.html
**Starting Price:** Free / ~$20/user/month (Core UCaaS plan)

**Overview:**
RingCentral Video is part of RingCentral's Unified Communications as a Service (UCaaS) platform, combining video meetings, team messaging, and business phone systems. For organizations replacing their entire telephony infrastructure, RingCentral bundles everything.

**Best For:**
- Businesses replacing traditional PBX phone systems
- Organizations that want video + phone + messaging from one vendor
- Companies scaling from SMB to mid-market who need UCaaS

**Strengths:**
- All-in-one UCaaS (video + voice + messaging + contact center)
- 99.999% uptime SLA
- 200+ integrations including Salesforce, HubSpot, and Microsoft 365
- AI-powered meeting transcripts and summaries
- Up to 200 participants on standard plans

**Limitations:**
- More complex and expensive than pure video conferencing tools
- Overkill for teams that only need video meetings

### 7. BlueJeans
**Website:** https://www.bluejeans.com/
**Starting Price:** ~$9.99/user/month (Standard, annual billing)

**Overview:**
BlueJeans (owned by Verizon) differentiates itself with Dolby Voice audio technology, which provides superior sound quality compared to most competitors. It supports mixed-mode meetings where some participants use the app and others dial in from a phone.

**Best For:**
- Teams where audio quality is paramount (executive briefings, earnings calls)
- Organizations mixing video and traditional dial-in conference calls
- Companies using Verizon's enterprise ecosystem

**Strengths:**
- Dolby Voice audio — best sound quality in the market
- Seamless blending of video participants and PSTN phone dial-ins
- Smart meetings with highlights, chapters, and action items
- Integrates with Slack, Microsoft Teams, and calendar tools

**Limitations:**
- Smaller market share means less universal name recognition
- Fewer integrations than Zoom
- Growth has slowed since Verizon acquisition

### 8. Whereby
**Website:** https://whereby.com/
**Starting Price:** Free (1 room, 100 min/meeting) / ~$6.99/month (Pro, annual)

**Overview:**
Whereby is the friction-free video tool — you create a permanent room URL (like whereby.com/yourname) and anyone clicks it to join, no download ever required. It's the most elegant solution for consultants, therapists, tutors, and freelancers who want a permanent, branded meeting room.

**Best For:**
- Freelancers, consultants, and therapists with recurring client meetings
- Educators and tutors who want a permanent, simple meeting room
- Developers embedding video calls directly into web apps (Whereby Embedded)

**Strengths:**
- Permanent, custom room URL (no scheduling required)
- Zero install — browser-based for all participants
- Whereby Embedded API for embedding video into any web app
- Clean, distraction-free interface
- Breakout groups and reactions available

**Limitations:**
- Limited to 100 participants (not suitable for large webinars)
- Fewer enterprise features than Zoom or Teams
- Paid plan required for more than 1 meeting room

---

### 9. Skype
**Website:** https://www.skype.com/
**Starting Price:** Free

**Overview:**
Skype, Microsoft's original video calling product, has faded from enterprise use but remains a widely recognized free option for personal calls, international voice calls (via Skype Credit), and small group meetings. It requires no account to receive calls via Meet Now links.

**Best For:**
- Personal calls and family video chats
- International calls via Skype Credit (low per-minute rates)
- Quick ad-hoc calls with people who already have Skype

**Strengths:**
- Entirely free for video and voice calls between Skype users
- Affordable international calling to landlines and mobiles (Skype Credit)
- Meet Now: generate a meeting link with no account required
- Screen sharing and file transfer included for free

**Limitations:**
- Declining user base — many contacts have migrated to Zoom or Teams
- No longer competitive with modern tools for business use
- Microsoft is prioritizing Teams over Skype for business users

---

### 10. Jitsi Meet
**Website:** https://meet.jit.si/
**Starting Price:** Free (always)

**Overview:**
Jitsi Meet is the open-source, self-hostable video conferencing platform. The public instance at meet.jit.si is completely free with no account required. For organizations that need to run video conferencing entirely within their own infrastructure, Jitsi is the go-to solution.

**Best For:**
- Privacy advocates who don't want any data leaving their servers
- Organizations that require full control over video infrastructure
- Developers building video features into their own applications

**Strengths:**
- 100% free and open-source
- No account required — share a link, anyone can join
- Self-hostable on your own servers
- End-to-end encryption option
- No participant limits when self-hosted

**Limitations:**
- Public instance can have performance issues under load
- No enterprise support (community support only for free version)
- Setup requires technical knowledge for self-hosted deployment
