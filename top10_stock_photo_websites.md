## Detailed Platform Profiles

---

### 1. Shutterstock
**Website:** https://www.shutterstock.com/
**Starting Price:** ~$29/month (10 images/month subscription)

**Overview:**
Shutterstock is the world's largest stock photo marketplace, with 450+ million assets including photos, vectors, illustrations, videos, and music. Its AI-powered search and Generative AI tools (text-to-image within the platform) have made it the first choice for creative teams that need volume and variety.

**Best For:**
- Marketing teams needing a large volume of diverse imagery
- Agencies with ongoing creative production pipelines
- Users who need photos + video + music from one subscription

**Strengths:**
- 450+ million assets (photos, vectors, video, music, 3D)
- Generative AI image creation built into the platform
- Batch download and team licensing options
- Deep Adobe Creative Cloud integration (plugin available)
- Contributor community ensuring fresh uploads daily

**Limitations:**
- Subscription pricing adds up for occasional users
- Standard license has usage limitations (enhanced license costs more)
- Some popular images are overused across marketing materials

---

### 2. Getty Images
**Website:** https://www.gettyimages.com/
**Starting Price:** ~$175 per image (Small, web resolution) for editorial use

**Overview:**
Getty Images is the premium standard for editorial, journalistic, and news photography. Its exclusive partnerships with major sports leagues, news agencies, and cultural events (Getty Images covers every major global event) make its library irreplaceable for editorial and publishing clients.

**Best For:**
- News publishers and editorial teams
- Sports and entertainment brands needing exclusive editorial imagery
- Advertising agencies that need truly premium, exclusive photography

**Strengths:**
- Exclusive imagery unavailable anywhere else (red carpets, sports events, breaking news)
- iStock by Getty Images for budget-conscious buyers
- Creative+ subscription for commercial use
- Highest editorial photography quality in the market

**Limitations:**
- Most expensive platform on this list
- Pricing model is complex (per-image vs subscription vs editorial licensing)
- Not cost-effective for high-volume routine marketing use

---

### 3. Adobe Stock
**Website:** https://stock.adobe.com/
**Starting Price:** ~$29.99/month (10 standard assets/month)

**Overview:**
Adobe Stock is the natural choice for teams already using Adobe Creative Cloud. Its integration with Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign allows designers to license images directly within the app they're working in — with watermarked previews that replace themselves upon purchase.

**Best For:**
- Graphic designers and creative teams using Adobe CC daily
- Teams that want seamless in-app licensing within Photoshop or InDesign
- Users who need photos, vectors, templates, and 3D assets in one place

**Strengths:**
- Seamless integration with all Adobe Creative Cloud apps
- License images directly from Photoshop, InDesign, and Premiere
- 400+ million assets including photos, vectors, video, 3D, and templates
- Generative Fill integration for AI-powered image editing

**Limitations:**
- Pricing is competitive but requires Adobe CC subscription for full value
- Some content is Creative Cloud exclusive (not available elsewhere)
- Less editorial/news photography than Getty

---

### 4. iStock
**Website:** https://www.istockphoto.com/
**Starting Price:** ~$22/month (10 credits/month)

**Overview:**
iStock (powered by Getty Images) sits in the mid-market tier — cheaper than full Getty licensing but with access to the same quality curation network. It uses a credit-based system with subscription options, making it ideal for buyers who need quality imagery without Getty's premium pricing.

**Best For:**
- Small businesses and solopreneurs who want premium quality without Getty prices
- Teams that buy images occasionally rather than in large monthly volumes
- Buyers who need Getty-quality editorial images at a mid-range budget

**Strengths:**
- Getty Images quality and curation at lower prices
- Mix of Signature (exclusive) and Essentials (standard) content tiers
- Credit packs for occasional use without monthly commitment
- Strong editorial collection from Getty's global network

**Limitations:**
- Credit system can be confusing (Signature images cost more credits)
- More expensive than Depositphotos or Dreamstime for volume buyers

---

### 5. Unsplash
**Website:** https://unsplash.com/
**Starting Price:** Free

**Overview:**
Unsplash revolutionized stock photography with a simple proposition: beautiful, professional-quality photographs freely available with no attribution required. Its community of 300,000+ photographers contributes images specifically intended for free commercial and personal use.

**Best For:**
- Bloggers, content creators, and startups on zero budget
- Designers who need authentic, editorial-style photography
- Website builders looking for non-generic imagery

**Strengths:**
- Genuinely free, including commercial use (Unsplash License)
- High editorial and aesthetic quality — curated, not clipart
- API for developers to integrate image search into apps
- Unsplash+: premium subscription (~$13/month) for exclusive, fully commercial images

**Limitations:**
- Popular images appear on many different sites simultaneously
- No exclusive licensing (everyone can use the same free images)
- Smaller library than paid platforms

### 6. Pexels
**Website:** https://www.pexels.com/
**Starting Price:** Free

**Overview:**
Pexels (owned by Canva) provides free photos and videos under a simple, permissive license — no attribution required, free for commercial use. Its video library sets it apart from Unsplash, making it a one-stop free resource for both photography and video content.

**Best For:**
- Social media managers who need both photos and video clips
- Bloggers and small business content creators
- Canva users (Pexels content is natively searchable within Canva)

**Strengths:**
- Free photos AND free video clips (Unsplash has no native video)
- Simple, consistent license (free commercial use, no attribution)
- Canva integration: Pexels is natively searchable in Canva
- Curated quality without requiring contributor accounts

**Limitations:**
- Not suitable for exclusive or premium licensing
- Smaller library than Shutterstock or Adobe Stock

### 7. Depositphotos
**Website:** https://depositphotos.com/
**Starting Price:** ~$9.99/month (10 images/month subscription)

**Overview:**
Depositphotos is the best value paid stock photo service, offering over 280 million assets at the lowest subscription price of any major commercial platform. It's particularly popular in Eastern European markets and as a budget-friendly alternative to Shutterstock.

**Best For:**
- Budget-conscious businesses that need paid, licensed imagery
- Small agencies managing multiple client visual assets
- Buyers who want large credit bundles at competitive prices

**Strengths:**
- Lowest subscription price among major paid platforms
- 280+ million photos, vectors, video, and audio assets
- Flexible plans (subscriptions and on-demand credit packs)
- Bulk download options for agencies

**Limitations:**
- Image quality and curation not at Getty or Shutterstock premium level
- Less brand recognition may concern clients paying for deliverables

---

### 8. Alamy
**Website:** https://www.alamy.com/
**Starting Price:** Pay-per-image (pricing varies by license)

**Overview:**
Alamy is a unique stock photography marketplace focused on editorial, niche, and archival imagery. It has one of the largest editorial photography collections outside Getty, with deep archives of historical, cultural, and news imagery unavailable on commercial platforms.

**Best For:**
- Publishers and journalists who need editorial and archival imagery
- Researchers needing historical or documentary photography
- Buyers seeking niche, authentic, non-generic commercial photos

**Strengths:**
- Massive editorial and archival collection
- Contributor-friendly platform (photographers keep 40–50% of each sale)
- Pay-per-image (no subscription required)
- Unique and less commonly used imagery compared to Shutterstock

**Limitations:**
- No subscription option — pay per image only
- Interface and search are less polished than competitors
- Pricing can be high for commercial-use images

---

### 9. Dreamstime
**Website:** https://www.dreamstime.com/
**Starting Price:** Free (limited) / Pay-per-image

**Overview:**
Dreamstime operates a hybrid model — a portion of its 250+ million asset library is available free (with attribution) while premium imagery is available via subscription or per-image purchase. It's a solid mid-tier option for buyers who want some free access alongside paid content.

**Best For:**
- Buyers who want to mix free and paid imagery
- Occasional buyers who don't want a monthly subscription commitment
- Content creators who are comfortable with attribution on free images

**Strengths:**
- Hybrid free + premium library model
- 250+ million assets
- Pay-per-image and subscription options
- Free content available (with Dreamstime attribution)

**Limitations:**
- Free images require attribution and have some usage restrictions
- Less polished user experience than Shutterstock or Adobe Stock
- Quality less consistent than top-tier platforms

---

### 10. Pixabay
**Website:** https://pixabay.com/
**Starting Price:** Free

**Overview:**
Pixabay offers a large library of free photos, vectors, illustrations, and videos — all released under the Pixabay License (no attribution required, free for commercial use). It's particularly strong for vector illustrations and graphics, making it a complement to photo-focused platforms.

**Best For:**
- Designers who need free vector illustrations and clipart
- Content creators who want variety (photos + vectors + video + music)
- Developers who need free assets for web and app projects

**Strengths:**
- 2+ million free photos, vectors, illustrations, videos, and music tracks
- No attribution required (Pixabay License)
- Strong vector illustration library (differentiator vs Unsplash)
- GIF and animated content available

**Limitations:**
- Quality is less curated and more variable than Unsplash or Pexels
- AI-generated images have increased in the library (mixed quality)
- Not suitable for exclusive or premium commercial licensing
