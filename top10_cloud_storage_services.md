## Detailed Service Profiles

---

### 1. Google Drive
**Website:** https://drive.google.com/
**Free Storage:** 15 GB (shared across Gmail, Drive, Photos)
**Paid Plans:** Google One — from ~$2.99/month (100 GB)

**Overview:**
Google Drive is the most widely used cloud storage service in the world, with over 3 billion active users. Its tight integration with Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Gmail makes it the default choice for anyone in the Google ecosystem.

**Best For:**
- Anyone using Google Workspace (Gmail, Docs, Sheets)
- Students and educators (Google Workspace for Education is free)
- Teams that collaborate on documents in real time

**Strengths:**
- Best-in-class real-time collaboration in Google Docs/Sheets
- Generous 15 GB free tier
- AI-powered search finds files by content, not just file name
- Available on all platforms via browser and native apps

**Limitations:**
- 15 GB is shared across all Google services (fills up fast with Gmail and Photos)
- Privacy concerns — Google scans content for advertising insights

---

### 2. Microsoft OneDrive
**Website:** https://onedrive.live.com/
**Free Storage:** 5 GB
**Paid Plans:** From ~$1.99/month (100 GB); bundled with Microsoft 365

**Overview:**
OneDrive is the cloud storage layer of Microsoft 365. Its deep integration with Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Windows makes it the default for corporate environments. Microsoft 365 subscribers get 1 TB of OneDrive storage included.

**Best For:**
- Microsoft 365 subscribers (1 TB included at no extra cost)
- Windows users who want seamless OS integration
- Corporate IT environments on the Microsoft stack

**Strengths:**
- Included free with Microsoft 365 subscriptions (1 TB)
- Native Windows integration (Files On-Demand, file explorer)
- Real-time co-authoring in Word, Excel, PowerPoint
- Personal Vault adds extra security layer for sensitive files

**Limitations:**
- Only 5 GB free without a Microsoft 365 subscription
- macOS and Linux experience significantly less polished than Windows

---

### 3. Dropbox
**Website:** https://www.dropbox.com/
**Free Storage:** 2 GB
**Paid Plans:** Plus plan ~$9.99/month (2 TB, billed annually)

**Overview:**
Dropbox invented the modern cloud sync experience and remains the gold standard for file synchronization across devices. Its selective sync, version history (180 days on paid plans), and LAN sync make it a favorite for creative teams and professionals.

**Best For:**
- Creative professionals syncing large files across multiple computers
- Teams that need reliable, fast file sync
- Users who work with design, video, or large media files

**Strengths:**
- The fastest, most reliable file sync engine in the market
- Paper (collaborative docs) and Replay (video review) built in
- LAN sync allows file transfer without using internet bandwidth
- 180-day version history on Plus and above

**Limitations:**
- Only 2 GB free (least generous of major providers)
- Paid plans are significantly more expensive than Google One or OneDrive

---

### 4. Box
**Website:** https://www.box.com/
**Free Storage:** 10 GB (individual)
**Paid Plans:** Personal Pro ~$10/month (100 GB)

**Overview:**
Box is positioned as the enterprise-grade cloud content management platform. Its compliance certifications (HIPAA, FedRAMP, FINRA), granular permissions, and Box Shield security tools make it the preferred choice for regulated industries.

**Best For:**
- Healthcare, finance, and legal industries requiring compliance
- Large enterprises with complex permission structures
- Teams that need workflow automation and e-signatures

**Strengths:**
- HIPAA, FedRAMP, FINRA, and ISO 27001 compliance
- Box Shield ML-based threat detection
- Box Sign e-signature tool built in
- Integrates with 1,500+ enterprise apps (Salesforce, Slack, Microsoft 365)

**Limitations:**
- Expensive for individual or small team use
- Interface feels less intuitive than Google Drive or Dropbox

---

### 5. pCloud
**Website:** https://www.pcloud.com/
**Free Storage:** 10 GB
**Paid Plans:** From ~$4.99/month (500 GB) or lifetime plans

**Overview:**
pCloud stands out with lifetime storage plans — a one-time payment for permanent cloud storage. It's Switzerland-based, offers client-side zero-knowledge encryption (pCloud Crypto), and delivers excellent desktop sync performance.

**Best For:**
- Users who prefer a one-time payment over subscriptions
- Privacy-conscious users who want zero-knowledge encryption
- Long-term storage without recurring fees

**Strengths:**
- Lifetime storage plans (one-time payment, no recurring fees)
- pCloud Crypto option for zero-knowledge client-side encryption
- Swiss-based (strong privacy laws)
- Backup from Facebook, Instagram, Dropbox, and Google Drive

**Limitations:**
- Zero-knowledge encryption (Crypto) costs extra
- Mobile app is slightly less polished than Google Drive

### 6. MEGA
**Website:** https://mega.io/
**Free Storage:** 20 GB (largest free tier on this list)
**Paid Plans:** From ~$5.38/month (400 GB)

**Overview:**
MEGA offers the most generous free storage tier of any major cloud provider — 20 GB — and applies end-to-end encryption to all files by default. Founded by Kim Dotcom, it has since evolved into a legitimate, security-focused service based in New Zealand.

**Best For:**
- Privacy-focused users who want E2E encryption by default
- Users who need a large free storage tier
- Anyone sharing large files securely

**Strengths:**
- 20 GB free storage (most generous free tier available)
- End-to-end encryption on all files and transfers by default
- Secure file sharing with password-protected and expiring links
- MEGA Chat and video calls built in

**Limitations:**
- Transfer quotas on free tier can limit downloading/sharing
- Desktop sync app can be resource-heavy

### 7. Tresorit
**Website:** https://tresorit.com/
**Free Storage:** None (21-day trial)
**Paid Plans:** From ~$10/month (1 TB, individual)

**Overview:**
Tresorit is the most security-focused cloud storage service on this list. All data is encrypted client-side before upload, meaning Tresorit itself cannot access your files. It holds multiple independent security audits and is trusted by law firms, healthcare providers, and financial institutions.

**Best For:**
- Professionals handling highly sensitive data (lawyers, doctors, journalists)
- Businesses in GDPR-regulated industries
- Users who want provably zero-knowledge storage

**Strengths:**
- Uncompromising zero-knowledge encryption (client-side)
- GDPR, HIPAA, and ISO 27001 compliant
- Detailed audit logs and access controls
- Secure sharing with expiring, read-only links

**Limitations:**
- No free tier
- More expensive than most competitors
- Limited third-party app integrations

---

### 8. iCloud Drive
**Website:** https://www.apple.com/icloud/
**Free Storage:** 5 GB
**Paid Plans:** From ~$0.99/month (50 GB) via iCloud+

**Overview:**
iCloud Drive is Apple's cloud storage layer, seamlessly woven into macOS, iOS, and iPadOS. For users who live inside the Apple ecosystem, it's invisible infrastructure — files, photos, and device backups sync automatically with no configuration needed.

**Best For:**
- iPhone, iPad, and Mac users who want seamless cross-device sync
- Apple ecosystem users (iCloud Photos, iMessage, Notes, Keychain)
- Users who want simple, effortless device backup

**Strengths:**
- Deeply integrated with all Apple devices and apps
- Automatic device backup included
- iCloud Private Relay (VPN-like feature) on iCloud+ plans
- Family Sharing allows up to 6 members to share storage

**Limitations:**
- Extremely limited usefulness outside the Apple ecosystem
- 5 GB free fills quickly with device backups
- Web interface is functional but not designed for heavy use

---

### 9. Backblaze B2
**Website:** https://www.backblaze.com/b2/
**Free Storage:** None
**Paid Plans:** ~$6/TB/month (pay-as-you-go)

**Overview:**
Backblaze B2 is a cloud object storage service aimed at developers, photographers, and businesses that need affordable bulk storage. At $6/TB/month, it's one-quarter the cost of Amazon S3. It's not a consumer sync drive — it's infrastructure-level storage accessed via API or integrated tools.

**Best For:**
- Developers building apps that need object storage
- Photographers and videographers storing large archives
- Businesses looking for cheap Amazon S3 alternatives

**Strengths:**
- One of the lowest prices for cloud storage at scale
- S3-compatible API (works with tools built for Amazon S3)
- Integrates natively with Cloudflare (free egress when used together)
- Personal Backup plans for consumer PC backup (~$9/mo unlimited)

**Limitations:**
- Not a consumer-facing sync tool (no desktop Drive experience)
- Requires technical setup for most use cases

---

### 10. IDrive
**Website:** https://www.idrive.com/
**Free Storage:** 10 GB
**Paid Plans:** ~$4.98/year for first year (10 TB)

**Overview:**
IDrive is primarily a PC backup service with strong cloud sync capabilities. It offers one of the best prices per terabyte in the industry and stands out with its IDrive Express feature — a physical hard drive shipped to you for offline data transfer.

**Best For:**
- Users who need to back up multiple PCs and servers
- Small businesses needing affordable multi-device backup
- Anyone with large amounts of data to upload (IDrive Express)

**Strengths:**
- Best price-per-terabyte for large storage plans
- Backs up unlimited devices on one account
- IDrive Express: physical hard drive mailed for large transfers
- Continuous backup mode monitors file changes in real time

**Limitations:**
- Interface is less polished than consumer-focused competitors
- Desktop app performance can be slow on older machines
